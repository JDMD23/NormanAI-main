# JD's target industries — the Norman target universe (his ICP taxonomy)

Provided by JD directly. This is the **qualification taxonomy** that defines which
companies Norman should target — used for **discovery** (where to source) and as a
**fit qualification + tier** (not a fine per-point scoring tilt — U4 keeps industry a
weak *score* component; this list is the *universe* definition). **Core (1–10) = primary
targets; Expansion (11–20) = secondary. Off-list = not a target** (exclude/deprioritize).
The overlay tags are cross-cutting attributes, not industries.

## CORE INDUSTRIES (primary targets)
1. **AI Infrastructure & Applications** — foundation models, AI agents, agent
   orchestration, inference, evaluations, model monitoring, synthetic data, voice AI,
   multimodal AI, AI search, AI safety, vertical AI applications.
2. **Cloud, Data & Developer Infrastructure** — databases, data pipelines, observability,
   APIs, integrations, developer tools, testing, CI/CD, cloud management, edge computing,
   workflow infrastructure.
3. **Semiconductors, Compute & Data Centers** — AI accelerators, memory, photonics, chip
   design, semiconductor equipment, networking hardware, data-center construction, power
   systems, cooling, workload optimization.
4. **Cybersecurity, Identity & Digital Trust** — cloud security, application security, AI
   security, identity management, fraud prevention, data security, privacy, compliance
   automation, threat intelligence, post-quantum security.
5. **Enterprise Software & Vertical SaaS** — finance ops, sales, marketing, customer
   support, procurement, HR, productivity, collaboration, industry-specific operating
   systems.
6. **Fintech & Financial Infrastructure** — payments, banking infrastructure, lending,
   insurance, wealth management, capital markets, treasury, accounting, fraud prevention,
   stablecoins, financial compliance.
7. **Healthcare Technology** — clinical workflows, revenue-cycle management, ambient
   documentation, diagnostics, patient engagement, care delivery, mental health,
   pharmacy, healthcare infrastructure.
8. ~~**Biotechnology & Life Sciences**~~ — **EXCLUDED (JD ruling, 2026-08-07).** Listed
   here originally in error. JD confirmed the existing hard exclusion is correct: biotech
   and therapeutics are **not** targets and route to Not a Fit, regardless of NYC
   headcount or hiring. This ruling settles the contradiction between this list and
   `EXCLUDED_INDUSTRIES` in the scorer — **the code was right, the list was wrong.**
   (Asked explicitly whether the exclusion was really about *wet-lab space* rather than
   the industry — i.e. whether biotech *software* should be kept — JD chose to exclude
   the whole vertical.)
9. **Robotics & Advanced Manufacturing** — industrial robots, humanoids, warehouse
   automation, computer vision, autonomous inspection, factory software, additive
   manufacturing, predictive maintenance.
10. **Energy, Climate & Grid Technology** — power generation, batteries, grid software,
    nuclear, geothermal, carbon management, energy efficiency, data-center energy,
    electrification, climate adaptation.

## EXPANSION INDUSTRIES (secondary targets)
11. **Defense, Aerospace & Space** — drones, autonomous systems, counter-drone, sensors,
    satellites, launch, geospatial intelligence, communications, defense manufacturing.
12. **Supply Chain, Logistics & Mobility** — freight, warehousing, procurement, fleet
    software, autonomous vehicles, last-mile delivery, aviation, maritime, transport
    infrastructure.
13. **Construction & the Built Environment** — construction software, A&E tools, building
    automation, modular construction, property operations, housing tech, infrastructure
    maintenance.
14. **Commerce, Retail & Marketplaces** — merchant infrastructure, commerce enablement,
    supply marketplaces, retail tech, pricing, inventory, fulfillment, recommerce.
15. **Legal, Regulatory & Government Technology** — legal AI, contract systems,
    compliance, regulatory intelligence, public-sector workflows, benefits admin,
    permitting, civic infrastructure.
16. **Education, Workforce & HR Technology** — AI tutoring, professional training,
    credentialing, recruiting, talent intelligence, workforce management, frontline-worker
    platforms.
17. **Consumer, Media & Gaming** — consumer AI, social products, creator tools,
    entertainment, gaming infrastructure, personalization, wellness, digital communities.
18. **Agriculture, Food & Water Technology** — precision agriculture, agricultural
    robotics, biological inputs, food production, alternative ingredients, cold-chain,
    water treatment, resource monitoring.
19. **Telecommunications, Networks & Edge** — network automation, private networks,
    satellite connectivity, edge infrastructure, spectrum technology, communications APIs.
20. **Crypto, Blockchain & Digital Assets** — stablecoin infrastructure, tokenization,
    custody, payments, blockchain developer infrastructure, decentralized identity,
    compliance.

## Hard exclusions (never targets, regardless of NYC signals)
- **Biotechnology, life sciences & therapeutics** (JD ruling 2026-08-07 — see core #8).
- Plus the standing exclusions already in the router: repped / CBRE conflict, too-big /
  enterprise, and any off-list vertical.

## How Norman should use this
- **Discovery:** source candidates from these verticals (core first).
- **Qualification:** in-list = a target; **off-list = not a target** (exclude or deep
  deprioritize, alongside the hard exclusions).
- **Tier, not fine tilt:** core > expansion as a coarse tier; among *qualified* targets,
  industry does **not** finely re-rank (concrete NYC demand does — U4). A great-fit AI
  company and a great-fit fintech excite JD equally.
- **Overlay tags** enrich the operator card and outreach angles; they are attributes, not
  gates.
